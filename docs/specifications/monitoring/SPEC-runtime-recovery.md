# Spec: runtime-recovery

> Derived from approved proposal D2–D6 / A03–A10, authorized 2026-09-21. D8/A13 was approved on 2026-09-23 and is being implemented; D9/A14 business intent is confirmed but its API is not yet approved. English canonical; paired Chinese copy. Depends on provider-contract.

## Connected Stage 2 delivery boundary — 2026-09-24

Production composition now opts both evaluators into initial durable `HOLD` in the canonical decision transaction and injects `CurrentDeliveryAdmission` into pending and direct dispatch. The dispatcher checks fresh committed authority before claiming a per-channel attempt and again after claim immediately before channel I/O. No SQLite transaction spans external I/O; prior uncertain-send retry semantics remain. A stored `ELIGIBLE` receipt alone never authorizes a later call. A bounded per-channel scan cursor lets held older alerts yield to later pending alerts.

Legacy official-risk admission verifies the original alert/result/activation provenance, exact current activation, calendar-certified completed official target, no missing or pending close, latest official risk stage, current risk row and proven complete inventory including opening balances. A resolved risk, later risk stage, proven deactivation/replacement or inapplicable exit is terminally suppressed with audit; an identical applicable repeated exit releases only its newest proven official-close occurrence. Unknown, corrupt, incomplete or over-budget evidence holds or surfaces a storage fault; it does not grant delivery. Explicit risk uses the latest matching rule result, current activation, completed five-minute target and generation-bound recovery barrier; actionable alerts additionally require a matching current payload and retain source cutoff + 90-second expiry. Unchanged rechecks leave the receipt revision/audit unchanged even when observation time advances. These facts are re-read for each channel, including after claim.

This is a locally verified code path, not approval for a real provider, external sender, user database migration or deployment. The earlier opt-in/future-dispatch paragraphs below describe the state of their dated increments; this section supersedes their implementation-status claims. Full recovery release gates and known unrelated suite failures remain tracked in the paired task index and acceptance report.
## Objective

One runtime owner can start at any time. After an interruption no longer than 120 hours it may reconcile eligible plans; after a longer interruption all active trading plans must expire before any recovery or alert dispatch. No trading decision may bypass exact dependency, activation or freshness guards.

## D5 controlled restart — 2026-09-24 decision

Configuration replacement uses safe stop, manual file edit and a new process start. Stop first blocks admission, then waits for physical provider requests and started notification sends with durable receipts before releasing storage and provider ownership. Failed draining retains ownership and forbids another instance on that database. A new process validates and freezes the whole configuration before provider or notification work; invalid configuration fails closed with zero such calls and may be corrected before another start. A relevant configuration change discovered while running pauses the old source and requires a safe stop/restart; supervision must not turn it into an automatic runtime or source replacement. Semantic-file metadata refresh alone does not require restart. Capture and recheck activation, semantic revision and runtime generation before commits and sends. The 120-hour outage clock uses only the legal local owner's durable heartbeat; provider/coordinator faults while that process remains alive are not outage, and importer activity or WebSocket pings cannot sign it.

## Five-day admission and proposed manual-sale amendment

- D8/A13: Record durable local owner liveness at bounded cadence, including standby/degraded operation; never use last startup or provider success as last-alive evidence. At startup, after storage opens but before active work, provider I/O or send admission, compare the last committed liveness with now. Exactly 120 hours remains recoverable; more than 120 hours expires all active plans of both families in one idempotent, audited transaction. Terminate their pending closes and suppress unsent alerts without deleting plans, holdings, confirmed trades, canonical results or audit. Missing/corrupt liveness or clock reversal blocks automatic admission pending operator review. Do not apply to a live database without approved migration and backup.
- Within 120 hours, resume after the latest attributable committed task node, acquire only interrupted market dates and evaluate their missing closes in order. Do not rerun all market history from activation. A long-lived plan needs a verified recent checkpoint; a short outage alone does not prove historical state. Unknown evidence blocks only the dependent plan.
- D9/A14: A buy/sell reminder links to the separate manual trade-registration area; the operator records an actual fill there only after a real trade. A direct alert-linked confirmation action and source-alert audit API remain unapproved follow-up work. Neither a risk warning nor read acknowledgment is a trade. If no trade is confirmed, inventory is unchanged; a confirmed sale uses the existing inventory-closure transaction with revision/idempotency and checked available quantity. Do not infer execution, backdate a confirmation, auto-order, or alter risk-warning/buy/sell-signal rules.

Recovery must not invent a target-lot prerequisite for a plan's buy/sell condition. The executable-plan `SELL` rule uses confirmed total sellable quantity, with no lot priority. The older legacy risk-exit path retains its previously specified preference among recorded lots; when its only sufficient holding is a confirmed opening balance, it may emit a quantity-bearing sell reminder without fabricating a lot, bucket, cost or acquisition date. A reminder never changes holdings; an actual sale is recorded separately against a real inventory source.

D8/A13 legacy resolution: If active plans exist without usable heartbeat evidence, automatic admission remains blocked. The only approved operator resolution is explicit, audited and retry-idempotent manual invalidation of those old plans, followed by creation/activation of new plans; never accept an operator-entered last-run timestamp as recovery proof. The exact version invalidated by either the >120-hour gate or manual legacy resolution cannot be reactivated, even if normal deactivation permits later reactivation. Preserve holdings and confirmed trades. The operator path is implemented for a missing heartbeat with active plans; corrupt/future evidence remains blocked for separate investigation.

For this exact condition, the local Web service remains available with `status=BLOCKED`, `readOnly=true`, `blockReason=LEGACY_PLANS_REQUIRE_REVIEW`. It never probes the provider, schedules monitor work, advances the heartbeat, recovers history or dispatches alerts. Ordinary HTTP mutations are rejected; only the reviewed maintenance POST is allowed. The system page previews at most 10 exact active symbol/version/revision identities; a separate confirmation submits their fingerprint. One SQLite write transaction rechecks absent heartbeat and unchanged identities, suppresses existing delivery eligibility, audits/removes pending official closes, deactivates every reviewed plan and appends a batch audit. Position lots, opening balances, confirmations and immutable plan JSON remain unchanged. A repeated confirmation before restart reuses the batch result. Changed identity, newly present heartbeat, inconsistent plan or partial failure rejects/rolls back the whole action. Restart the monitor before creating new plans; empty-plan admission then establishes new heartbeat evidence.

The following existing contract applies to eligible plans and remains unchanged by D8; D9's API still requires review.
## Contract

- Local configuration/storage bootstrap precedes provider work. A coordinator persists through expected dependency errors, including degraded startup and unknown calendar; those errors never consume the three-crash supervisor budget.
- A degraded startup remains supervised and retries with a 30/60/120/300-second capped sequence; `degradedReason` and `nextRetryAt` expose a stable stage and the next attempt. A missing local Tushare configuration or incompatible stock semantic contract produces a distinct provider error code without leaking credentials or filesystem errors. An aged provider observation is not `READY`, but a still-valid durable semantic contract may permit guarded work; each actual market response remains independently validated.
- Reconciliation executes small coalesced scope work units, one owner, bounded queue 32. Round-robin eligible recovery work prevents a failing scope starving siblings. A maintenance wake is at most 30 seconds, without unconditional provider probes.
- Transient retry uses 30/60/120/300 seconds; shared provider cooldown is at least 300 seconds; one permission recheck per operation per 300 seconds, including across process replacement. Missing credentials cause no physical requests.
- Keep physical concurrency 1, starts at least 1.25 seconds apart, timeout 15 seconds, immediate retry 0, backoff keys at most 64. Cancellation does not free a physical slot until its worker actually exits.
- Freeze completed cutoff using authoritative exchange calendar: pre-first-minute requires no nonexistent current-day minute; lunch retains morning coverage; after close reconstructs due obligations; closed days use previous completed data. Unknown calendar schedules retry, never fabricates trading dates.
- Readiness requires exact persisted required coverage. Empty/partial/current-day-only responses do not fill old dates; future rows cannot satisfy an earlier cutoff. Target rollover recomputes work.
- Discover missed legacy official closes from eligible exchange dates minus committed close identities, including absent CLOSE_PENDING records. Respect actual activation/revision; replay chronological transitions. Unknown required historical state blocks the affected plan.
- Reference keys include plan family, required data, activation and semantic revision, not merely symbol/date. Captured generation/activation must still match at commit.
- Both plan families preserve immutable evaluation identities. Store delivery eligibility atomically with each result/outbox transaction, outside canonical hashes. Unsent actionable intraday alerts expire after source cutoff + 90 seconds.
- Risk notices are state-validity based, not blanket 90-second expiry. A per-plan recovery barrier holds risk delivery until that plan reaches the current target, then rechecks latest risk/activation immediately before send. HOLD is not terminal suppression. Historical obsolete alerts remain auditable; external uncertain sends cannot guarantee exactly once.
- Shutdown stops admission and joins owned tasks/workers and started send/receipt work before closing persistence. Invalid startup configuration blocks work; running configuration drift pauses the old source until safe stop and new process start, without fallback or automatic replacement.
## Injected incremental interfaces

- `RecoveryCoordinator` owns one `run(stop=...)` invocation for its entire lifetime. It synchronously reads local `requirements(now)`, claims one `RecoveryTicket`, awaits one bounded `execute(ticket)`, rereads requirements, then accepts only the still-current result. These callbacks do not authorize evaluation or delivery. `wake()` is an event-loop-local coalesced notification, cleared before the next local snapshot.
- `execute` returns `READY`, `RETRY` or `BLOCKED`; `PENDING` is internal schedule state, not a completed-step result. Retry deadlines that elapsed during work or a clock jump defer to the next 30-second maintenance wake instead of causing a crash/spin. Each unit yields to the event loop. The interruptible wait has a separate monotonic 30-second ceiling in addition to the injected wall-clock deadline.
- Requirements revisions must change for every relevant activation, configuration/credential availability, dependency or semantic change. Unchanged `READY`/`BLOCKED` requirements remain coalesced; a maintenance tick alone is not a permission reset. Expected provider failures are typed results; storage and programming faults remain faults.
- Stop prevents the next admission and drains the directly awaited callback. Cancellation propagates through that callback and prevents coordinator reuse. It does not claim that a provider thread has exited: lifecycle must still close admission and await request-controller `aclose()` before closing persistence or replacing the runtime.
- `RecoveryService.recover_scope` acquires at most one symbol for a frozen `RecoveryTarget`, at most 240 required minute identities and a committed-coverage reader. It rechecks time before physical start and readiness, filters future rows before persistence, and reads back exact committed coverage. A current-day endpoint cannot fill earlier dates. Its `ready` is minute evidence only, not plan/business readiness. `PERSISTENCE_FAILED` must enter lifecycle fault handling, not an endless provider retry.
- If a local guard rejects a queued target before provider I/O, `RequestNotStartedError` propagates through request control without recording provider success or failure. Existing backoff counts and permission blocks must survive this abort; target rollover is not evidence of provider recovery.
- These interfaces remain injected/offline slices until RR010–RR014 and the alert gates are complete. They do not enable production recovery or dispatch historical alerts.

### Missing-close discovery increment

`discover_missed_closes` is a pure, injected, per-plan discovery function, not an executor or database authority reader. `MissedCloseScope` binds the legacy plan, version, actual activation revision/time and one observation time. Scan at most 32 calendar days per call, starting by default at actual activation; `next_window_start` only identifies the next window. A later requested window does not certify the omitted prefix. Neither a finished window nor all `PROVEN` rows mean business recovery is complete or sending is allowed. Results describe that frozen observation, not a durable progress checkpoint: recertify changed activation/evidence and rescan affected dates as new obligations become due.

Intersect the plan's historical activation policy with authoritative exchange-open dates and actual activation no later than the 15:00 source cutoff. The close becomes due at the existing first scheduled attempt, 15:20, including equality. An expired policy can still owe historical closes. Never infer weekend closure or use a missing pending marker as completion evidence. Missing calendar or completion evidence stays `UNKNOWN`; storage/programming exceptions propagate.

`OfficialCloseCompletion` distinguishes certified `ABSENT`, attributed `PROVEN` committed occurrence and `UNKNOWN`. Bind evidence to the exact official-close identity, actual activation and observation. A proven occurrence requires a full matching result and the adapter's committed-publication certification; a bare hash-only claim or another activation's claim is not absence and cannot become proven. This pure function validates bindings, not durable publication/activation provenance. A claimed blocked result still does not prove complete historical business state.

Discovery preserves date order and marks a possible causal-history gap when later non-absent evidence follows an absent close, or a proven occurrence follows unknown earlier evidence within the window. The caller must also establish continuity across windows before any replay. Existing evaluation uses current inventory/risk, a monotonic decision head, and recovery write times in risk transitions; simply replaying sorted dates through it is not historical reconstruction. Durable evidence readers, causal-state reconstruction, execution and production composition remain separate RR009 work. No provider request, transaction, notification or production default is changed by discovery.

### Durable missing-close evidence increment

`LegacyCloseRecoveryReader.discover` composes the scanner with SQLite evidence in one fresh, owned read snapshot. It derives the legacy scope from the current active pointer, an `ACTIVE` canonical plan version, and the complete bounded activation event/audit chain; it never accepts a caller's guessed activation. It shares the existing chain and monitor high-water validation with the legacy alert-origin reader. Missing/inactive/explicit/invalid scope returns typed unknown rather than a certified empty result. New evidence cannot postdate the observation. Storage faults propagate, and no caller state is flushed or committed.

The close, alert-origin and single-lot readers share an owned-session preflight. Require a fresh Session whose default bind is one application-configured SQLite Engine; every queried model's mapper and table-clause bind must resolve to that same Engine. Reject direct or routed Connection bindings, split-engine routing, unbound sessions and pre-used sessions before entering the context or issuing SQL. Rejection must not flush, commit, roll back or close caller-owned state. Misconfigured factories raise instead of becoming evidence `UNKNOWN`. The application's file-backed engine and explicit-BEGIN hooks remain prerequisites; this guard does not certify arbitrary custom engines or Session overrides.

The reader obtains the bounded window's persisted SSE calendar; missing or malformed rows remain unknown, with no weekday fallback. Each exact close claim is checked against its complete canonical result and deterministic snapshot publication. A publication must belong to the derived current activation interval, not merely match the plan hash. Hash-only claims, incomplete/corrupt/oversized results and wrong-activation publications remain `UNKNOWN`. A proven occurrence does not certify position-lot history, historical business readiness or permission to replay/send.

To certify `ABSENT`, both claim and result must be absent and an orphan-publication check must find no contradictory evidence. The lazy per-symbol snapshot-publication scan reads at most 1000 rows plus one overflow sentinel, once per window. It recomputes canonical event IDs using each publication's result hash and the requested identity, without a recovery-write-time filter. Malformed, oversized or overflowing publication history keeps otherwise absent candidates unknown; it does not erase a directly proven exact occurrence. This conservative fallback can be exhausted by ordinary long-running intraday history. Durable direct provenance remains necessary before production rollout can promise progress beyond this proof budget; do not silently increase or remove the bound. Activation events and audits retain their independent 1000-row proof budgets.

All evidence belongs to that database snapshot and observation only. Re-read after activation, calendar, target or generation changes and before later mutation/admission. Historical-state reconstruction, cross-window continuity, chronological execution and production integration remain separate work. The existing alert-origin reader continues to prove original attribution even after later deactivation or reactivation; sharing its validation does not turn origin proof into current delivery permission.

### Position-lot mutation evidence increment

The version 1 snapshot fields are fixed: `id`, `symbol`, `bucket`, `quantity`, `remaining_qty`, `acquired_trade_date`, `cost_fen`, `related_alert_id`, `closed_at`. A future DTO shape change must not silently redefine this stored version.

Future `POSITION_BUY_RECORDED` and `POSITION_REDUCTION_RECORDED` audits retain an additive `recoveryEvidence` object in their existing details. Its `schemaVersion` is 1; `effectiveTradeDate` is the normalized accepted acquisition/reduction date, not the audit write date. `before` is null for a buy and the complete previous `PositionLotRecord` JSON snapshot for a reduction; `after` is the complete resulting snapshot. Snapshot fields use the existing record's snake_case names and JSON encodings. Existing top-level `bucket`, `quantity`, `symbol`, action/entity metadata, lot hash algorithm, monitor-event identities and mutation rules remain unchanged.

This records accepted business facts, not an independently certified exchange session or a complete historical inventory. `created_at` remains the audit write time; `closed_at` remains the existing full-closure write time. A delayed or out-of-order registration does not make these times equivalent to `effectiveTradeDate`. Audit evidence, lot mutation, monitor event and inventory revision remain in the same transaction. Rollback leaves none of that mutation committed, and an application idempotency replay does not append another audit. No additional API field, migration, provider request or notification is introduced.

Old audit rows are neither rewritten nor guessed. Their missing source date/full state remains unknown to future historical reconstruction. Before consuming new evidence, a later reader must validate its version, action/entity bindings, exact before/after hashes and mutation invariants, then independently establish per-lot ordering and complete inventory coverage. The old hashes cover lot snapshots, not the new date/envelope: they are consistency checks, not authentication of the effective date. The writer alone grants no historical-state, replay or delivery permission. Opening balances, inventory-confirmation history, risk source-effective dates, legacy evidence decoding and chronological reconstruction remain separate work.

### Position-lot evidence decoding increment

`decode_position_lot_mutation` is a pure bounded decoder of one audit's action, entity type/ID, before/after hashes, details JSON and write timestamp. It returns an immutable validated mutation or typed `UNKNOWN`, distinguishing missing legacy evidence, unsupported version and invalid content. `VALID` means internally consistent mutation evidence only, not committed publication, authenticated effective date, complete lot/inventory history, chronological ordering or replay/send permission. It performs no database, provider or notification operation.

Before parsing, require text details bounded to 16384 UTF-8 bytes. Reject duplicate JSON keys, nonstandard numeric constants, unexpected/missing fields, coerced numbers/booleans, invalid dates and naive timestamps. Version 1 uses exactly the writer's fixed snapshot fields, existing accepted identifiers/symbols/buckets, positive quantities/cost and valid remaining/closure state. Do not impose UUIDv4, a fixed 100-share board lot, equality of write/effective dates, or equality of full-closure and audit timestamps. The timestamp contract validates representation, not clock monotonicity or exchange-calendar authority.

Lot/entity IDs accept 1–128 non-padding characters, including non-UUID IDs from existing custom ID factories; related alert IDs retain the repository's 1–36 bound. Padding is conservatively invalid, not silently trimmed. This does not widen mutation admission: the existing lot lookup/reduction limit remains 36. Numeric fields, including the version, require actual integers rather than booleans, floats or numeric strings. Dates must round-trip to the same ISO date; timestamps use the existing ISO datetime representation or its UTC JSON `Z` form, not permissive alternate separators/basic dates. Check the character bound before allocating UTF-8 bytes; malformed Unicode and excessive JSON nesting return invalid evidence.

Require lot identity, symbol and bucket to agree across row metadata, top-level details and both snapshots. Recompute the existing lot hashes without changing their representation or timestamp offsets. A buy has no before state/hash, initial remaining quantity equals its positive operation/original quantity, no closure, and effective date equals acquisition date. A reduction starts from an open lot, preserves every immutable field, reduces remaining quantity by exactly the positive operation quantity and has an effective date after acquisition; zero remaining requires a closure timestamp and positive remaining forbids one. Missing old evidence is never filled from write time. A later database reader must bound rows before loading, validate committed chain/current-row/publication agreement and establish coverage separately.

### Durable single-lot history increment

`PositionLotHistoryReader.read(lot_id, max_mutations=256)` owns a fresh application SQLite session and one explicit-BEGIN read snapshot. The limit is a strict integer from 1 to 1000; invalid arguments or a non-fresh factory session raise, storage failures propagate, and missing/unsupported/invalid evidence or exhausted history budgets return typed `UNKNOWN`. A frozen `PROVEN` result carries the current lot, every mutation in hash-chain order, corresponding publication sequences and the snapshot's monitor high-water mark. It establishes only the stored history of this one lot, not complete inventory, authenticated source dates, historical applicability, or replay/send permission.

The factory must bind all proof tables to the same application-configured SQLite engine, not an externally owned connection/transaction or routed database. Reject invalid bindings before any proof query without flushing, committing, rolling back or closing the caller's transaction. A new Session object alone does not prove transaction ownership or committed visibility.

Read every audit for the exact lot/entity scope without filtering actions. Require exactly one buy root and a unique connected chain consuming all records, strict positive reductions, and agreement with a strictly decoded raw current row. Reject duplicates, branches, orphans, gaps and a mismatched tip. Do not sort by audit ID, write time or effective date; source dates may go backwards between reductions because existing admission permits late registration. Ordered execution must resolve source-effective semantics separately.

Read all publications for the aggregate, not a filtered recent replay page. Require a one-to-one match to the existing deterministic position event IDs, exact canonical stored payload/identity, event type/schema, symbol/status and audit occurrence time. Their positive sequences must increase along the hash chain and not exceed the snapshot high-water mark; unrelated global sequence gaps are allowed. An aggregate-ID collision with other event types is conservatively unknown. The seven-day replay window does not delete stored events and is not a proof cutoff. Actually missing publications remain unknown, even when the audit and current row agree.

Both history queries fetch at most the limit plus one overflow sentinel. Details are bounded to 16384 UTF-8 bytes and event payloads to 4096; SQL truncation and storage-type/length checks must precede decoding so oversized values or embedded NUL cannot hide trailing data. Bound metadata/current-row text as well and avoid ORM date/type coercion. Add non-unique indexes through `0020_lot_history_indexes`: audit `(entity_type, entity_id)` and monitor `(aggregate_id, sequence)`. These support scoped lookup rather than whole-table scans; the migration changes no records and downgrade removes only these indexes. Test upgrade, repeated upgrade, downgrade preservation, metadata parity and query plans in temporary databases only. No live migration, new writer, production consumer or default switch is included.

### Inventory writer evidence increment

This writer-only RR009 increment preserves accepted future facts for manual inventory confirmation, opening-balance creation/reduction and automatic confirmation synchronization. It does not reconstruct history, authenticate an effective date, establish completeness at an earlier cutoff or authorize replay/delivery. Old records remain untouched. Admission, response shapes, existing hashes, monitor events, ID-factory consumption and clock calls remain unchanged; no migration or production recovery switch is included.

Confirmation snapshots use exactly these stored column names: `symbol`, `confirmation_id`, `as_of_trade_date`, `confirmed_at`, `inventory_revision`, `total_qty`, `sellable_qty`, `included_lot_ids_json`, `opening_balance_ids_json`, `complete_registration`. Dates use ISO date strings; stored timestamp and member-JSON strings are copied unchanged, not parsed/recomputed through `InventorySnapshot`. Null denotes no confirmation, not zero inventory. Opening snapshots use exactly `id`, `symbol`, `original_qty`, `remaining_qty`, `sellable_from`, `confirmation_id`, `revision`, `closed_at`; do not fabricate acquisition date or cost.

Extend only future `POSITION_INVENTORY_CONFIRMED` details with `recoveryEvidence`: `schemaVersion` 1, `previousInventoryRevision`, `nextInventoryRevision`, full confirmation `before`/`after`, and `createdOpeningBalance` (full snapshot or null). Preserve existing detail fields and the existing null before-hash/partial after-hash. Capture the old confirmation before overwriting it, including stale confirmations. A repeated accepted confirmation is a new revision, while an application idempotency replay creates no new evidence. No new opening-balance creation event is invented.

Extend only future `POSITION_OPENING_BALANCE_REDUCTION_RECORDED` details with `recoveryEvidence`: `schemaVersion` 1, accepted `effectiveTradeDate`, accepted `boardLot`, and full opening `before`/`after`. Preserve existing quantity/symbol and before/after hashes. New opening snapshots copy ORM strings unchanged, while legacy opening hashes retain their existing DTO serialization (for example, stored `+00:00` versus DTO `Z`). Keep the existing same-day sellability and per-operation `quantity // boardLot` revision increment: board lots may change between operations, and the remaining balance need not be divisible by that board lot. The accepted source date may differ from write/closure time and may go backwards across registrations.

For each lot buy, lot reduction and opening-balance reduction, append one separate `POSITION_INVENTORY_CHANGED` audit under `POSITION_INVENTORY`, scoped to the symbol. Its details contain only `recoveryEvidence`, with `schemaVersion` 1, `sourceAudit` (`id`, `action`, `entityType`, `entityId`), accepted `effectiveTradeDate`, signed `totalDelta`, `syncOutcome` (`SYNCED`, `MISSING` or `STALE`), and `before`/`after` objects each containing `inventoryRevision` and a full `confirmation` snapshot or null. Record the actual revision advance even when no confirmation is synchronized. `SYNCED` preserves the current quantity/clamping behavior without refreshing confirmation time/date/member references; `MISSING` and `STALE` preserve their existing no-op confirmation behavior.

The added inventory audit's hashes are SHA-256 over its respective canonical before/after objects (UTF-8, sorted keys, compact separators, non-ASCII preserved). Its ID is `uuid5(NAMESPACE_URL, "trade-plan-monitor:inventory-recovery:v1:" + source_audit_id)`, so no additional application ID is consumed; collision must fail visibly, never replace or ignore an existing row. Its write time is copied from the source audit. The strict version 1 lot-evidence format remains unchanged; extra inventory facts never enter its entity scope or envelope.

All added evidence shares the existing caller transaction with lots, balances, confirmation, inventory revision, existing publications and application idempotency receipt. No independent commit or extra clock read is allowed. A failure rolls back the command through the existing unit-of-work boundary. Snapshot fields are explicitly enumerated, not inferred from future ORM/DTO fields. Do not impose a new writer size limit from the single-lot reader's 16384-byte budget: existing accepted member lists remain accepted. Bounded readers, source verification and complete inventory-history proof are separate follow-ups; storing this evidence alone grants no authority.

### Historical recovery integration boundary

The user resolved V1a's temporal choice: an alert is not an execution. Before human confirmation keep the original holding; after confirmation subtract only the confirmed quantity. Historical recovery uses the registered state at that cutoff. A D1 sale registered on D3 does not reduce D1/D2 holdings retroactively. Use the persisted manual mutation's audit write timestamp as registration time, inclusively at the cutoff; `effectiveTradeDate` retains settlement/source meaning but cannot move confirmation earlier. A later whole-inventory confirmation likewise cannot establish an earlier account balance. No new confirmation screen or inferred trade is introduced; accepted repository buy/reduction commands remain the mutation boundary.

Three integration obligations apply regardless of that choice: keep opening balances distinct from legacy `PositionLotSnapshot` values (do not invent bucket, cost or acquisition identity); persist the same proven historical lot records used for evaluation, not a fresh current-lot read; derive input-only risk effective time from the attributable transition-producing official-close result's source cutoff, not recovery write time or a later ordinary `WAIT`. Preserve canonical result bytes and existing audit writes, and recheck inventory/risk/activation revisions before commit. Only a provably absent suffix after valid prior state is a candidate for replay; a missing D1 before already committed D2 is a causal hole, not permission to rewrite results.

### Confirmed-time inventory history reader

`PositionInventoryHistoryReader.read(symbol, cutoff, observed_at)` owns one fresh application SQLite snapshot. Require aware times and `cutoff <= observed_at`; reject invalid caller arguments/session bindings before taking ownership. Return immutable `ProvenInventoryHistory` or typed `UnknownInventoryHistory`. The proven value contains historical open `position_lots`, separate `opening_balances`, the confirmation evidence at that revision (possibly null), selected `inventory_revision`, `current_revision`, monitor high-water sequence and both times. It proves the registered ledger, not an unconfirmed brokerage account or a replay/send permit. Legacy rules may consume proven lots without whole-account confirmation; explicit rules still require their applicable confirmation and certified calendar-derived sellability.

Rebuild from revision 0 through every consecutive inventory audit to the current revision, checking exact confirmation chaining and each source audit's ID/action/entity, quantity, effective date, write time and lot/opening before/after state. Every scoped source audit and position publication must be accounted for; source publication sequences increase across all entities. Confirmation creation has no invented publication. At each whole-inventory confirmation verify exact then-open member sets, total and the writer's asserted sellability arithmetic; do not repeat those membership checks against later synchronized confirmation metadata, whose member lists intentionally remain unchanged. Finally compare all current rows, including closed holdings, and the raw current confirmation to the reconstructed tip. Never use current rows as substitutes for missing history.

Registration instants must be nondecreasing in revision order; ties are valid and all changes at the cutoff are included. A reversed clock chain is `UNKNOWN`, not sorted into a different history. No required record or closure can be later than `observed_at`; selected facts cannot have future effective/acquisition/sellable dates relative to the Shanghai target date. A cutoff before the first proven registration can yield an empty registered ledger with null confirmation, not a proven empty account. Example: 1000 shares registered on D1, 300 sold on D1 but confirmed on D3 => D2 reads 1000, D3 after confirmation reads 700; reopen/repeated reads remain 700 and do not write another deduction.

Fixed proof budgets are 256 inventory mutations, 256 distinct ledger entities and 8388608 cumulative fetched text bytes. Stream bounded SQL projections and check the aggregate budget before decoding/retaining each row. Fetch one extra row to detect exhausted row budgets; return `HISTORY_LIMIT`, never truncated proof. Missing, malformed, unsupported or temporally inconsistent evidence remains `UNKNOWN`; storage faults propagate. Add index-only migration `0021_inventory_history_index` after `0020_lot_history_indexes` for `ix_position_lot_symbol`; existing audit/entity and publication/aggregate indexes cover other scoped reads. Migration/reopen tests use temporary databases only. Histories beyond these proof budgets need a later durable-checkpoint/provenance path before unrestricted production recovery; this reader is not that path.

### Connected legacy missed-close transaction

The connected `LegacyHistoricalCloseReader.prepare(symbol, trade_date, observed_at)` owns one fresh application SQLite snapshot covering active-plan provenance, the unfinished close suffix, registered inventory and prior risk. At every plan age, the latest proven committed close is the task checkpoint; completed decisions are not recomputed. Stored earlier transition checkpoints, including their immediate completed predecessors, are read only as needed to verify the current risk chain and its source time; intervening ordinary `WAIT` closes are not re-evaluated. `discover_missed_closes` still scans at most 32 calendar days per call, but the reader pages consecutive windows to the observation date, so 32 days is not a plan-expiry, recovery-admission or checkpoint-selection limit. Each bounded window reads its SSE calendar rows once within the same snapshot; missing or malformed days remain unknown. Existing inventory/history proof budgets still apply. It accepts only the earliest absent close in a suffix: unknown occurrences, causal holes, later committed occurrences or incomplete enumeration return typed unknown. Same-content risk from an earlier activation without attributable transition evidence stays unknown. Cross-activation provenance remains a follow-up before unrestricted recovery.

Current-plan risk requires a unique committed official-close transition with matching risk fields/revision, validated claim/publication/activation/source cutoff, and the current `RISK_STATE_UPDATED` audit's exact hash, details and write time. A transition source requires `market_data_at` equal to its 15:00 cutoff, only risk transitions and transition time equal to evaluation time; persisted write time may differ. Later ordinary `WAIT` results must preserve those risk fields; their times cannot replace the transition source. Supply an input-only risk copy whose `updated_at` is that transition's source cutoff; leave stored result bytes and audit times unchanged. Missing risk may seed `CLEAR` revision 0 only with no scoped transition or orphan risk audit. Different-plan current risk may seed `CLEAR` with its physical revision only when prior scoped results are compatible initial clear snapshots. Initial snapshots may legitimately vary their prior close price; never reset the physical revision.

An opt-in historical official-context wrapper carries the evaluated historical lots, current inventory revision, exact current risk row (or absence), actual activation revision and monitor high-water sequence. It requires `recovery_delivery=True`. The evaluator commit checks all fences even for `WAIT`, persists the same historical lot records used by evaluation, and writes any alert with initial `HOLD` in the existing transaction. The high-water fence conservatively retries after concurrent publications, including another close that did not change risk. Failed fences roll back result, state transition and alert together. No provider call or send occurs within that transaction, existing canonical hashes are unchanged and production composition remains unchanged.

Preflight the target before stock daily fetching and close-pending preparation, so an out-of-order request cannot leave a later pending date that blocks the earlier obligation. In the single-plan official entry point, also preflight an unclaimed historical target before shared index preparation; a duplicate claim may follow the existing index path. Reconstruct the proof again after market persistence; never cache the early proof for commit. The multi-plan batch still needs a per-plan chronological admission policy before production recovery integration. If the historical inventory contains opening balances, this legacy path remains unavailable: it must neither silently omit their quantity nor invent legacy lot attributes. The complete opening-aware consumer is still required before unrestricted recovery.

The first scoped risk transition at physical revision greater than 1 must link its audit `before_hash` to the preceding revision audit's `after_hash`; missing or malformed predecessor evidence is unknown. A prior committed `BLOCKED_DATA` occurrence cannot prove completed causal risk state. A newly evaluated historical `BLOCKED_DATA` result is not claimed or stored as a completed close; required data may recover and the target may retry.

### Inventory record validation for connected recovery

`decode_inventory_mutation` validates one confirmation or source-linked inventory-change audit before a history consumer uses it. Inputs include the audit ID, action/entity metadata, existing hashes, raw details and write timestamp. Return frozen `ValidatedInventoryMutation` with previous/next inventory revisions and raw-preserving confirmation snapshots, or `UnknownInventoryMutation` with `MISSING_EVIDENCE`, `UNSUPPORTED_VERSION`, `INVALID_EVIDENCE` or `EVIDENCE_LIMIT`. `VALID` proves row consistency only, never committed source, complete history, historical applicability or send permission. No writer, schema or production-default change is included.

Bound the outer details to 262144 UTF-8 bytes and each embedded member-JSON string to 65536 bytes before parsing; each member list allows at most 1000 unique non-padding IDs, each 1–128 characters. These are reader limits, not new writer admission rules. Preserve member JSON and aware timestamp strings exactly; parsed dates and member tuples are immutable. Timestamps allow at most 64 characters; confirmation/source/audit IDs at most 128. Reuse opening-snapshot validation and its existing ID bounds. Reject duplicate object keys, extra/missing fields, nonstandard constants, malformed Unicode, excessive nesting and scalar coercion; an exceeded byte/member budget returns `EVIDENCE_LIMIT` rather than truncation. Confirmation snapshots require explicit `complete_registration=true`, nonnegative integer quantities/revision and sellable quantity no greater than total. Synchronized snapshots may have odd quantities.

Confirmation audits retain their null before-hash and existing partial after-hash. Require revision advance by one, after-confirmation revision/date/write timestamp bound to the envelope, and newly asserted total/sellable quantities divisible by 100. Prior stale confirmation is retained, not silently repaired. Optional created opening balance binds the symbol, new confirmation ID, confirmation date and listed opening ID, with original equal to positive remaining quantity, revision zero and no closure; it does not prove that all listed holdings exist. Do not require confirmation IDs to change across accepted confirmations.

Inventory-change receipts require the exact deterministic source-derived ID and canonical before/after hashes. Bind the source action to its entity type and the delta sign; bind the envelope to the symbol and consecutive revisions. `SYNCED` changes only confirmation revision, total by the signed delta and sellable by the existing minimum rule; `MISSING` preserves null; `STALE` preserves the entire mismatched-revision confirmation. Reuse raw strings in these comparisons. No date ordering is inferred from the write timestamp. Exact source-row agreement, publication, complete revision/member chains and temporal applicability remain obligations of the connected reader; neither decoding nor a later confirmation retroactively certifies an earlier close.

### Opening-balance reduction decoding increment

`decode_opening_balance_mutation` is a pure decoder of one `POSITION_OPENING_BALANCE_REDUCTION_RECORDED` audit under `POSITION_OPENING_BALANCE`. Return immutable `ValidatedOpeningMutation` (`VALID`) or `UnknownOpeningMutation` (`UNKNOWN`, reason `MISSING_EVIDENCE`, `UNSUPPORTED_VERSION` or `INVALID_EVIDENCE`). Inputs are action, entity type/ID, before/after hashes, details JSON and write timestamp. Preserve raw validated write/closure timestamp strings in output; decode dates to immutable dates. This proves only single-record consistency, not authenticated dates, committed source/publication, complete inventory, historical applicability or replay/send permission. No I/O, writer change, migration or production consumer is included.

Bound details to 16384 UTF-8 bytes, checking character length before encoding and byte length before parsing. This is a local budget for the fixed two-snapshot shape, not a confirmation/member-list budget or writer admission limit. Exceeding a bound yields `INVALID_EVIDENCE`. Opening/entity IDs use 1–36 non-padding characters, matching reduction lookup; confirmation IDs use a separate conservative 1–128-character reader bound and need not be UUIDs. Timestamps use at most 64 characters and the existing aware ISO datetime or UTC `Z` representation; hashes are exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Reject malformed Unicode, duplicate JSON keys, nonstandard numeric constants, excessive nesting, extra/missing fields and number/boolean coercion. Well-formed legacy quantity/symbol details without `recoveryEvidence` are missing evidence, never reconstructed from write time; an integer version other than 1 is unsupported.

Version 1 requires the exact writer fields. Snapshot quantities/revisions and operation quantity/board lot are actual integers, not booleans. Original quantity is positive, remaining quantity lies from zero to original, and revision is nonnegative. Bind both snapshot IDs/symbols to row metadata/top-level symbol. Require an open positive before balance, exact quantity subtraction, positive board lot dividing the operation quantity, and revision increment `quantity // boardLot`. Only remaining quantity, revision and closure may change; the after balance is zero exactly when it has an aware closure timestamp. Effective trade date is no earlier than `sellable_from`, allowing same-day reductions. Do not impose original/remaining quantities divisible by the current board lot, a fixed 100-share unit, a global revision/quantity formula, or ordering/equality between write, closure and effective dates.

Recompute existing opening hashes through `OpeningBalanceRecord.model_dump(mode="json")` and canonical UTF-8 SHA-256, including its UTC `+00:00` to `Z` normalization; never hash raw new snapshots in place of the old DTO encoding. Raw strings remain unchanged in the result. Reuse existing strict parsing primitives without changing the lot decoder. Confirmation and inventory-change receipt decoding, source/chain proof, member budgets and historical completeness remain separate increments.
