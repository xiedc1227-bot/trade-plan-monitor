# Spec: runtime-recovery

> Derived from approved proposal D2–D6 / A03–A10, authorized 2026-09-21. English canonical; paired Chinese copy. Depends on provider-contract.
## Objective

One runtime owner remains able to reconcile local and provider facts after arbitrary restart; no trading decision may bypass exact dependency, activation or freshness guards.
## Contract

- Local configuration/storage bootstrap precedes provider work. A coordinator persists through expected dependency errors, including degraded startup and unknown calendar; those errors never consume the three-crash supervisor budget.
- Reconciliation executes small coalesced scope work units, one owner, bounded queue 32. Round-robin eligible recovery work prevents a failing scope starving siblings. A maintenance wake is at most 30 seconds, without unconditional provider probes.
- Transient retry uses 30/60/120/300 seconds; shared provider cooldown is at least 300 seconds; one permission recheck per operation per 300 seconds, including across process replacement. Missing credentials cause no physical requests.
- Keep physical concurrency 1, starts at least 1.25 seconds apart, timeout 15 seconds, immediate retry 0, backoff keys at most 64. Cancellation does not free a physical slot until its worker actually exits.
- Freeze completed cutoff using authoritative exchange calendar: pre-first-minute requires no nonexistent current-day minute; lunch retains morning coverage; after close reconstructs due obligations; closed days use previous completed data. Unknown calendar schedules retry, never fabricates trading dates.
- Readiness requires exact persisted required coverage. Empty/partial/current-day-only responses do not fill old dates; future rows cannot satisfy an earlier cutoff. Target rollover recomputes work.
- Discover missed legacy official closes from eligible exchange dates minus committed close identities, including absent CLOSE_PENDING records. Respect actual activation/revision; replay chronological transitions. Unknown required historical state blocks the affected plan.
- Reference keys include plan family, required data, activation and semantic revision, not merely symbol/date. Captured generation/activation must still match at commit.
- Both plan families preserve immutable evaluation identities. Store delivery eligibility atomically with each result/outbox transaction, outside canonical hashes. Unsent actionable intraday alerts expire after source cutoff + 90 seconds.
- Risk notices are state-validity based, not blanket 90-second expiry. A per-plan recovery barrier holds risk delivery until that plan reaches the current target, then rechecks latest risk/activation immediately before send. HOLD is not terminal suppression. Historical obsolete alerts remain auditable; external uncertain sends cannot guarantee exactly once.
- Shutdown stops admission and joins owned tasks/workers before closing persistence. Invalid replacement configuration blocks affected work, never silently falls back.
## Injected incremental interfaces

- `RecoveryCoordinator` owns one `run(stop=...)` invocation for its entire lifetime. It synchronously reads local `requirements(now)`, claims one `RecoveryTicket`, awaits one bounded `execute(ticket)`, rereads requirements, then accepts only the still-current result. These callbacks do not authorize evaluation or delivery. `wake()` is an event-loop-local coalesced notification, cleared before the next local snapshot.
- `execute` returns `READY`, `RETRY` or `BLOCKED`; `PENDING` is internal schedule state, not a completed-step result. Retry deadlines that elapsed during work or a clock jump defer to the next 30-second maintenance wake instead of causing a crash/spin. Each unit yields to the event loop. The interruptible wait has a separate monotonic 30-second ceiling in addition to the injected wall-clock deadline.
- Requirements revisions must change for every relevant activation, configuration/credential availability, dependency or semantic change. Unchanged `READY`/`BLOCKED` requirements remain coalesced; a maintenance tick alone is not a permission reset. Expected provider failures are typed results; storage and programming faults remain faults.
- Stop prevents the next admission and drains the directly awaited callback. Cancellation propagates through that callback and prevents coordinator reuse. It does not claim that a provider thread has exited: lifecycle must still close admission and await request-controller `aclose()` before closing persistence or replacing the runtime.
- `RecoveryService.recover_scope` acquires at most one symbol for a frozen `RecoveryTarget`, at most 240 required minute identities and a committed-coverage reader. It rechecks time before physical start and readiness, filters future rows before persistence, and reads back exact committed coverage. A current-day endpoint cannot fill earlier dates. Its `ready` is minute evidence only, not plan/business readiness. `PERSISTENCE_FAILED` must enter lifecycle fault handling, not an endless provider retry.
- If a local guard rejects a queued target before provider I/O, `RequestNotStartedError` propagates through request control without recording provider success or failure. Existing backoff counts and permission blocks must survive this abort; target rollover is not evidence of provider recovery.
- These interfaces remain injected/offline slices until RR010–RR014 and the alert gates are complete. They do not enable production recovery or dispatch historical alerts.

### Missing-close discovery increment

`discover_missed_closes` is a pure, injected, per-plan discovery function, not an executor or database authority reader. `MissedCloseScope` binds the legacy plan, version, actual activation revision/time and one observation time. Scan at most 32 calendar days per call, starting by default at actual activation; `next_window_start` only identifies the next window. A later requested window does not certify the omitted prefix. Neither a finished window nor all `PROVEN` rows mean business recovery is complete or sending is allowed. Results describe that frozen observation, not a durable progress checkpoint: recertify changed activation/evidence and rescan affected dates as new obligations become due.

Intersect the plan's historical activation policy with authoritative exchange-open dates and actual activation no later than the 15:00 source cutoff. The close becomes due at the existing first scheduled attempt, 15:20, including equality. An expired policy can still owe historical closes. Never infer weekend closure or use a missing pending marker as completion evidence. Missing calendar or completion evidence stays `UNKNOWN`; storage/programming exceptions propagate.

`OfficialCloseCompletion` distinguishes certified `ABSENT`, attributed `PROVEN` committed occurrence and `UNKNOWN`. Bind evidence to the exact official-close identity, actual activation and observation. A proven occurrence requires a full matching result and the adapter's committed-publication certification; a bare hash-only claim or another activation's claim is not absence and cannot become proven. This pure function validates bindings, not durable publication/activation provenance. A claimed blocked result still does not prove complete historical business state.

Discovery preserves date order and marks a possible causal-history gap when later non-absent evidence follows an absent close, or a proven occurrence follows unknown earlier evidence within the window. The caller must also establish continuity across windows before any replay. Existing evaluation uses current inventory/risk, a monotonic decision head, and recovery write times in risk transitions; simply replaying sorted dates through it is not historical reconstruction. Durable evidence readers, causal-state reconstruction, execution and production composition remain separate RR009 work. No provider request, transaction, notification or production default is changed by discovery.

### Durable missing-close evidence increment

`LegacyCloseRecoveryReader.discover` composes the scanner with SQLite evidence in one fresh, owned read snapshot. It derives the legacy scope from the current active pointer, an `ACTIVE` canonical plan version, and the complete bounded activation event/audit chain; it never accepts a caller's guessed activation. It shares the existing chain and monitor high-water validation with the legacy alert-origin reader. Missing/inactive/explicit/invalid scope returns typed unknown rather than a certified empty result. New evidence cannot postdate the observation. Storage faults propagate, and no caller state is flushed or committed.

The reader obtains the bounded window's persisted SSE calendar; missing or malformed rows remain unknown, with no weekday fallback. Each exact close claim is checked against its complete canonical result and deterministic snapshot publication. A publication must belong to the derived current activation interval, not merely match the plan hash. Hash-only claims, incomplete/corrupt/oversized results and wrong-activation publications remain `UNKNOWN`. A proven occurrence does not certify position-lot history, historical business readiness or permission to replay/send.

To certify `ABSENT`, both claim and result must be absent and an orphan-publication check must find no contradictory evidence. The lazy per-symbol snapshot-publication scan reads at most 1000 rows plus one overflow sentinel, once per window. It recomputes canonical event IDs using each publication's result hash and the requested identity, without a recovery-write-time filter. Malformed, oversized or overflowing publication history keeps otherwise absent candidates unknown; it does not erase a directly proven exact occurrence. This conservative fallback can be exhausted by ordinary long-running intraday history. Durable direct provenance remains necessary before production rollout can promise progress beyond this proof budget; do not silently increase or remove the bound. Activation events and audits retain their independent 1000-row proof budgets.

All evidence belongs to that database snapshot and observation only. Re-read after activation, calendar, target or generation changes and before later mutation/admission. Historical-state reconstruction, cross-window continuity, chronological execution and production integration remain separate work. The existing alert-origin reader continues to prove original attribution even after later deactivation or reactivation; sharing its validation does not turn origin proof into current delivery permission.

## Explicit risk delivery rule — approved 2026-09-21

The user explicitly approved latest-state delivery for an unsent `RISK_ALERT`: after recovery catches up, still-valid risk may be delivered; resolved risk is retained but suppressed; insufficient facts remain on nonterminal HOLD. This does not change the evaluator or create a new alert intent.

An injected pure classifier uses the original validated `ExplicitAlertEvent`, authoritative current plan/activation, latest validated `ExplicitEvaluationResult`, requested completed target, runtime generation and typed recovery-barrier evidence. `active=None` means authoritative absence, never a failed lookup. Missing/unreadable authority must not be represented as absence. The barrier binds symbol, plan hash, activation revision, generation and exact target, with no pending state-changing obligation and reconciliation through that target. This value is an interface for future durable barrier reconstruction, not proof that such persistence already exists.

| Ordered check | Delivery result |
|---|---|
| Authoritatively inactive/replaced/reactivated/expired plan or mismatched original rule/action | `SUPPRESS`, retain audit reason; do not extend the legacy cross-day exception to explicit plans |
| Missing/mismatched/incomplete barrier, stale generation/target, absent latest result or future evidence | `HOLD`, never terminal suppression |
| Current result does not exactly match the active plan/hash/revision/symbol/target/rule/action | `HOLD` |
| Selected risk rule has unknown or contradictory blockers beyond its condition/safety evidence | `HOLD` |
| Either required condition/safety root outcome is `MISSING_INPUT` | `HOLD`, including a known-false root paired with an unknown root |
| Otherwise either required condition/safety root outcome is `UNSATISFIED` | `SUPPRESS`, risk no longer qualifies |
| All required root outcomes are `SATISFIED` | `ELIGIBLE`; final durable activation/barrier/state recheck still belongs immediately before send |

Absent safety evidence is neutral only if the active rule declares no safety condition. Preserve the evaluator's existing three-valued tree semantics: an `ALL` root may be false despite a missing child, and an `ANY` root may be true despite a missing alternative. Delivery does not recursively override those root outcomes. The evaluator's corresponding `CONDITION_UNSATISFIED`, `SAFETY_UNSATISFIED`, `CONDITION_INPUT_MISSING`, `SAFETY_INPUT_MISSING` blockers follow their evidence outcomes, not an unconditional HOLD. Other rules' BUY/SELL blockers do not redefine this risk rule. `ONCE_ALREADY_EMITTED`, `TARGET_ALREADY_EMITTED` and empty `signal_intents` concern repeat emission, not current risk resolution. Eligibility is separate from canonical result/alert bytes and hashes. Durable barrier reconstruction and dispatch integration remain separately gated.

## Legacy risk delivery rule — approved 2026-09-21

The user approved delivering only old official-risk notices still applicable to the latest recovered risk state and current target inventory. Notices superseded by later states remain auditable but are not replayed one by one. This approval does not alter risk/price/indicator formulas or authorize real notification delivery.

- Recover all due official-close obligations chronologically before deciding applicability. An empty pending-close table does not prove recovery is complete. Missing origin activation attribution, required historical evidence, current inventory or calendar authority means `HOLD`, not a guessed current state.
- Bind the original event to its committed result and actual activation revision. Legacy payloads do not carry that revision, so matching the plan hash alone is insufficient. Authoritatively inactive, replaced or reactivated identities are suppressed; unreadable identity remains on `HOLD`.
- Preserve the existing cross-day exception only for `RISK_BREACH`: the same active identity, event creation date later than `trading_date`, and activation eligible on that historical date. Neither exit notice acquires that exception.
- A resolved (`CLEAR`) risk or a different breach episode suppresses the old notice. A known later exit-ready, exit-pending or recovery stage supersedes the earlier breach notice. A complete ordinary `WAIT` must not resurrect that earlier stage or by itself prove risk resolution. In particular, `RISK_EXIT_READY` does not necessarily update `RiskState`; checking only `risk != CLEAR` is unsafe.
- Exit delivery must still refer to the same applicable cause, target lot, bucket and quantity. Check authoritative current inventory and settlement facts using the existing evaluator's lot preference; never retarget an old notice or substitute a new quantity. Proven exhausted/replaced/inapplicable targets suppress; unknown applicability remains on `HOLD`.
- The user additionally approved latest-only delivery for repeated identical `RISK_EXIT_READY` instructions across trading dates. Within the same proven plan/activation, breach cause, target lot, bucket and quantity, a newer committed official-close occurrence supersedes the earlier unsent occurrence. Retain earlier records with a supersession audit reason; deliver at most the newest still-applicable instruction after the recovery barrier. Order by original official trade date, not recovery write time. This does not merge different causes, lots, quantities or activation revisions, and does not authorize delivery when applicability is unknown.
- The implementation must distinguish a complete result supporting the instruction from missing or blocked evidence. A missing legacy `signal_intent` alone is not a general risk-resolution test. An intraday `WAIT` is not a replacement official-close result. A later official-close `WAIT` without a proven applicable exit remains on `HOLD`; it does not automatically preserve or suppress that exit. An origin result and a different result for the same canonical official-close identity cannot both be authoritative. Do not infer source-day ordering from risk `updated_at`, which can reflect recovery evaluation time. Historical attribution and current-applicability evidence still require reviewed predicates before the legacy classifier or dispatcher is enabled.

This section records the approved business rule and source constraints. Separate pure explicit and legacy classifiers implement their respective policies using injected authority; legacy current-evidence reconstruction, durable suppression and final send admission are still open work.

### Legacy current-applicability interface

`classify_legacy_risk_delivery` is a pure injected policy, not a database reader or sender. It takes proven/unknown original provenance, authoritative active identity (including typed unknown, distinct from `None` absence), `LegacyCurrentEvidence`, `LegacyInventoryEvidence` and the recovery barrier. `origin_plan` plus `origin_result` rebuild the original payload before any permanent suppression; invalid original evidence must remain `HOLD`, even if the current identity differs.

The future reader must certify the requested completed target as current for injected `now` using the authoritative exchange calendar. Active/current/barrier/inventory `observed_at` values all equal that one logical read timestamp; callers must reread and recertify, not relabel an old snapshot. A newly read closed-day snapshot may retain the previous session target; there is no blanket risk age limit. The caller certifies the latest due official date, complete committed history without omitted closes, and the latest risk stage retained across ordinary `WAIT`. Contradictory canonical results or unknown/blocked evidence remain `HOLD`.

Inventory must be current, complete and selected by the existing evaluator's lot preference, not copied from historical alert/result positions. `settlement_trade_date` is the calendar-certified applicable exchange session at `now`, bounded by target date and current date. Acquisition cannot postdate that certified session, including on closed days. Sellability must agree with `acquired_trade_date < settlement_trade_date`; contradictory T+1 facts remain `HOLD`. Repeated exit supersession requires separate proven occurrence identity/instruction, a newer official source date and publication no later than the current/barrier observation. These typed values express the reader's obligations; the pure classifier does not establish calendar truth, reconstruct durable current history or grant channel permission itself.

### Legacy origin evidence interface

A read-only origin reader may return `PROVEN` only when existing committed records agree on the original plan version/hash, official-close evaluation identity/result hash, rebuilt alert semantics, and activation interval. Use persisted monitor-event sequence ordering, corroborated by activation audit, rather than wall-clock ordering alone. Verify alert and result publication belong to the same activation interval; same-hash reactivation is a different origin. Validate the durable `MonitorSequence.last_sequence` and reject relied-on sequences above its high-water mark. The original activation must also satisfy the existing evaluator cutoff invariant: it cannot follow the official 15:00 source cutoff or evaluation time. This reader proves origin only, never current delivery eligibility.

The reader owns a fresh application-configured session and one read snapshot; it never flushes or commits caller state. Corroborate activation/deactivation events and audits in both directions, with contiguous revisions and a chain tip matching current activation-state/active-plan pointers, so a missing interval boundary or trailing history cannot make the latest remaining event look authoritative. These current pointers establish chain completeness, not a replacement origin. Cap each symbol's activation-event and activation-audit collection at 1000 rows, reusing the existing replay row budget but not its time horizon; detect one extra row and return `UNKNOWN` rather than treating a truncated chain as complete. Durable direct provenance is the later path for histories beyond that bounded proof.

Missing, conflicting or unparseable historical evidence returns typed `UNKNOWN` evidence for the delivery adapter to hold; storage failures remain visible faults, not authoritative absence. Migration `0006` did not reconstruct historical activation events, `0007` did not reconstruct complete activation history, and `0009` may retain hash-only evaluation claims without a full result. Do not manufacture provenance or rewrite immutable identities for those records. The monitor replay query horizon is not proof that older persisted events were deleted. This slice is read-only and offline-injected; additive provenance persistence and verified backfill remain separate work.

## Storage and rollout

### Delivery metadata increment

`0019_alert_delivery_eligibility` adds a separate `alert_delivery_eligibility` row keyed by the existing event ID. It never rewrites alert/result payloads, hashes, acknowledgements or channel attempts. Missing metadata means unclassified `HOLD`, never permission. An injected session-bound repository initializes rows on demand or in batches of at most 100; it does not backfill all history during schema upgrade. Initialization is idempotent and never replaces a prior classification.

Delivery class follows the signal, not severity: legacy BUY/pressure-reduction and explicit BUY/SELL use `INTRADAY_ACTIONABLE`; the three legacy official-risk signals and explicit `RISK_ALERT` use `STATE_RISK`. Source cutoff is the original explicit target or legacy `market_data_at`; an absent legacy cutoff remains unknown and is never replaced with evaluation/creation time. Actionable expiry is exactly source cutoff + 90 seconds and is enforced at equality; risk has no time-only expiry. Initial rows remain `HOLD`, even within the freshness window.

The isolated repository can persist the existing explicit-risk classifier's decision and its captured `RecoveryBarrierEvidence`, using injected authoritative inputs. An `ELIGIBLE` row is a historical classification receipt, not a durable send permit; restart/generation change, target advance and current authority must be rechecked by the later dispatcher. Legacy rows cannot become `ELIGIBLE` through this explicit-only path. Origin persistence and legacy applicability integration remain separate work; dedup hits must never acquire the current activation's provenance.

Every change compares the expected revision, increments it and appends an `AuditLog` record in the same caller-owned transaction. Suppression is terminal for that original event; HOLD is reversible. The repository neither commits nor rolls back caller transactions. Writers serialize before reading; an already-open stale snapshot or database failure stays a visible fault. Corrupt stored metadata is not interpreted as missing/eligible. Downgrade refuses to discard any delivery metadata, including suppression audit state. The schema's empty down/up path and interrupted upgrade are tested only on temporary databases.

`AlertOutbox.enqueue_recoverable` is an opt-in caller-transaction helper for alert plus initial HOLD creation; existing enqueue and dispatcher defaults are unchanged. Dedup preserves existing metadata, and rollback preserves any pre-existing canonical alert. Reads revalidate source/class/expiry against a freshly loaded canonical alert, closed status/reason combinations, and complete bound five-minute barrier/result evidence for both `RISK_TRUE` and `RISK_FALSE`. Persisted receipts cannot silently borrow another activation's evidence. Future dispatch must avoid HOLD rows starving later candidates at the query limit, recheck authority per channel after claim and before I/O, and preserve existing uncertain-send semantics. No SQLite transaction is held over channel I/O, and no exactly-once external-delivery guarantee is introduced. See ADR 0007 for the storage decision and rollback boundary.

Additive provider recovery checkpoints and delivery metadata only. Migration 0018_provider_recovery follows verified head 0017_market_index_recovery; later delivery migration follows the then-current head. Bounded safe keys/reasons, no secrets. Refuse downgrade that would discard pending state. Temporary-database migration tests are authorized; live migration, backup and deployment require separate approval. Do not enable new default until all alert, lifecycle and transport gates pass.
### Opt-in evaluator transaction increment

`RuntimeEvaluationService` and `ExplicitRuntimeEvaluationService` accept a strict keyword-only `recovery_delivery: bool = False`. `True` selects `AlertOutbox.enqueue_recoverable` at the existing legacy intraday, legacy official-close and explicit new-alert write sites. Initial HOLD metadata and audit share the decision's existing unit-of-work transaction with its claims, results, transitions and alert events. Failure rolls that decision transaction back and must not publish its decision/alert events. Earlier committed market facts or close-pending preparation remain durable; they are not part of the failed decision transaction.

The option adds storage only. It neither classifies current risk nor authorizes or enforces channel delivery. Production factories/composition do not enable it; the existing dispatcher does not yet honor this metadata. Do not connect this isolated opt-in path to a real sender until RR013 and rollout gates pass. `False` preserves existing behavior and hashes. Duplicate evaluations remain read/no-op paths, and legacy intent-dedup skips remain skips: they must not rewrite prior suppression, backfill attribution or assume the current activation owns an earlier event. Missing historical metadata still needs the separately bounded initialization/reconstruction path. No intent means no delivery row. Commit-time activation and mutable safety guards remain unchanged.

## Structure and style

Small typed functions in runtime/reconciliation.py and runtime/recovery_targets.py; reuse scheduler, request controller, evaluator transactions and SQLite repositories. No general workflow engine. Python 3.14, explicit enums, immutable targets.

`.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pytest tests/unit/test_runtime_reconciliation.py tests/unit/test_recovery_targets.py tests/integration/test_runtime_recovery_storage.py -q`
Then all proposal section 7 gates. Fake clocks/providers/channels, temporary DB, actual process restart tests. Preserve identity goldens and existing failures until diagnosed.
## Boundaries

Always enforce scoped exact evidence and stale-alert safety. Ask before changing evaluator rules, adding dependencies or live actions. Never suppress failing tests, expose credentials or restore an old DB over new business records.
